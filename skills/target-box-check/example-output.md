*Fictional account. Figures invented to show the format.*

**Unit economics:** break-even CPA £120 per survey-booked lead (20% survey to job × £1,500 gross margin × 40% willing to spend)
**Objective stated:** maximum surveys from a fixed £4,500 monthly budget

## Detail
### Flat Roofs
Spend was at the £60 cap on 26 of 30 days and 31% of impressions were lost to budget, so budget is the binding constraint. Before 17 August the campaign delivered at £58 against a £90 target; in the last 30 days it has delivered at £81. That is consistent with Google's description of the rollout, though not proof of it. At £1,800 a month, a rise from £58 to £81 is 31 surveys down to 22. The objective is maximum surveys from the budget, the signal is sound, and there has been no bidding change in 30 days. Recommend an experiment with Maximise conversions and no target, judged after two conversion cycles (8 weeks at a 28-day lag).

### Repairs
Not budget-limited, CPA sitting on the £70 target, and impression share lost to rank up from 18% to 27%. The target is the limit. Break-even is £120, so the target is turning away surveys the business would pay for. But this campaign also counts phone taps as primary conversions (see below), so the £69 is partly cost per tap. Fix the signal first; once the call action is secondary, recommend moving the target to £95 and reviewing in two cycles.

## Fix before you change any target
"Click to call (website)" is primary in Repairs and Emergency and counts every tap. Set it to secondary first; otherwise both campaigns are steering toward taps, and any target is steering toward the wrong thing.

## Limits of this check
Performance before and after 17 August is a comparison, not a controlled test. Survey-booked data for the last 28 days is still incomplete.
